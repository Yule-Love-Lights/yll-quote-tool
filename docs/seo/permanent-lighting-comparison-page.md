## Why this page exists, and why it's separate

In permanent lighting, customers search brand names as if they were product
categories — the way people say "Sheetrock" for drywall. Someone who wants what
we sell may well type a competitor's brand name, because that's the only word
they've heard for it.

That traffic is real and it converts, because the searcher has already decided
they want the product and is only choosing a vendor.

It gets its own page, and does not go on the service page, for three reasons:

1. **Search intent is different.** The service page serves "I want this
   installed." This page serves "I'm comparing systems." Mixing them weakens both.
2. **Trademark posture.** Naming competitors on a page that is genuinely
   comparative and factual is normal and defensible. Sprinkling their brand
   names through your own service page to catch traffic is not, and it reads as
   spam to customers and to Google alike.
3. **It ages differently.** Product specs change. Keeping them in one page means
   one page to re-verify, not a claim buried in the service copy.

**Target URL:** `/permanent-roofline-lighting-comparison/`
(top-level, not under `/services/` — it is a guide, not a service)

**Target searches:** `jellyfish lights alternative`, `permanent christmas lights

## A hard constraint on the content

Every comparison of these brands I could find is written by a **dealer for one
of them** — the manufacturer's own blog, or a regional installer. Their prices,
warranty terms, and "which app is better" verdicts are marketing claims, not
verified facts, and several of them contradict each other.

**None of those numbers are in the copy below, and none should be added without
checking the manufacturer's own current published page.** A comparison page that
misstates a competitor's warranty is how a useful page becomes a legal letter —
and it's just as bad if it's merely out of date.

So the draft compares on **attributes a homeowner can verify with their own
eyes**, and it makes specific claims only about Yule Love Lights, which you can
substantiate.

## Page copy

### H1
Permanent Roofline Lighting on Long Island: How to Compare Systems

### Intro

If you've been looking into permanent lighting, you've probably run into a few
brand names and come away unsure whether you were comparing products or just
comparing marketing.

Here's the honest version. Most permanent roofline systems are built the same
way: a track mounted under the roofline or fascia, individually addressable RGB
LEDs inside it, and an app to control them. The real differences are narrower
than the marketing suggests — and the ones that matter most to how you'll feel
about the system in year five have less to do with the brand on the box than
with how it was installed and who picks up the phone when something goes out.

We install permanent lighting on Long Island. So we have a side. What follows is
what we'd tell you to check anyway.

### H2 — What actually differs between systems

**How visible the track is when it's off.**
This is the thing people regret. Systems differ in how the channel sits against
the fascia and whether the diodes face out toward the street or down toward the
ground.

The detail that decides it is whether the track follows a line your house
already has. Mounted tight under the fascia or along an existing trim band, it
reads as part of the trim. Mounted proud of the surface, it reads as a strip
someone added — and no amount of color at night fixes that in the daylight.

Ask to see a photo of a real installed home with the system **off** — not a
rendering, not a night shot with everything lit. Any installer who can't produce
one is telling you something.

**How the light throws at night.**
Outward-facing diodes read as visible dots of color from the street. Downward-
facing ones wash the face of the house and read more like architectural
lighting. Neither is wrong; they're different looks, and people have strong
preferences once they see both. Ask for a night photo of each.

**Color capability.**
Nearly all of these systems do millions of colors. What varies is whether a
single run can show **multiple colors at once** — a red section and a blue
section on the same continuous roofline, rather than the whole house being one
color at a time. That's the difference between a system that can do a flag on
the Fourth and one that can only do red.

The other thing to check is whether the warm white looks genuinely warm or
slightly blue. Warm white is the setting most homeowners actually live on for
eleven months of the year, so look at that one hardest.

**The app.**
Every one of them has an app. Ask to hold a phone and drive it before you buy.
Check specifically: how many taps to change one color, whether scheduling is
automatic by sunset, and whether it works without the house Wi-Fi.

**Who installs it, and who services it.**
Most of these brands are licensing and dealer networks, not crews. The company
that comes to your house is a local installer either way. So the question isn't
really which brand — it's which local company, and whether they'll still be
around and still be picking up in year five.

### H2 — The questions worth asking any installer

- Can I see a daytime photo of a home you installed?
- Can I see that same home at night?
- Who does the warranty work — you, or do I call a national number?
- What happens if a section goes out in December?
- Are you licensed and insured in New York, and can I see it?
- Is the quote itemized, and is takedown or service included?

### H2 — Where we fit

We're Yule Love Lights. We're family-owned, based on Long Island, and we've been
lighting homes here since 2022 — Nassau and Suffolk, Elmont and Great Neck out
to Montauk.

A few things that are true about us and worth weighing:

- **We're local, and service is us.** If something goes out, the people who
  installed it are the people who come fix it. There's no national dispatch
  number in the middle.
- **We do the whole year.** Permanent lighting, Christmas installs, landscape
  lighting, event lighting. Most homeowners who go permanent still want
  landscape work or a wreath-and-garland package, and that's one vendor and one
  crew rather than three.
- **We're licensed, insured, and CLIPA-certified.**
- **Our systems run multiple colors on the same roofline at once** — not one
  color per house. *(Photographed on a real install; see the photo notes below.)*
- **Typical permanent roofline systems run $2,500–$5,000** for small-to-medium
  homes, installed. We'll quote your actual roofline rather than a per-foot
  guess.

### H2 — Is permanent lighting right for you?

It's worth it if you want your house lit for more than Christmas — Halloween,
the Fourth, Diwali, a birthday, or just warm white on an ordinary Tuesday in
March. It pays for itself against seasonal installs somewhere around year four
or five, depending on the size of your display.

It's *not* the better choice if what you love is the classic C9 look with
garland, wreaths, and lit trees. Permanent systems do rooflines beautifully;
they don't do the rest of a designed display. Plenty of our customers have both.
