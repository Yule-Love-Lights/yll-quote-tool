## The core problem this rebrand solves

You are running three visual brands at once:
1. **Cartoon fair look** (van, trailer, tent, tablecloth, crew shirts, flyers, yard sign): bright red and green, cartoon houses, snow-cap letters, clip-art bulbs. Friendly and local, but not premium. This is the one dragging you down-market.
2. **Editorial lookbook** (the 2025 Canva lookbook): crimson, cream, gold foil, magazine serif, real night photos. Genuinely premium.
3. **Website** (yulelovelights.com): navy and gold, dark, polished, real photos, 166 five-star reviews, big commercial clients (Chick-fil-A, Marriott, CVS, UBS Arena), media logos.

Two of the three are already good. The rebrand is not about talent, it is about picking one look and making everything obey it. The premium two win; the cartoon gear catches up to them.

---

## The recommended direction (one paragraph)

Vintage American Christmas hospitality. Premium but warm. Crimson, cream, and gold as the palette, real night photos of real homes as the imagery, one retro script wordmark plus an editorial serif as the type. Everything the customer sees says the same thing the strategy says: the best-looking house on the block, handled entirely for you, guaranteed, so you never touch a ladder or worry about a dark bulb in December.

## Decisions and recommendations captured this session

### Positioning (locked earlier, unchanged)
Not lights. A done-for-you display and a feeling. Chick-fil-A hospitality on the outside of your home.

### Name
Fully locked. Yule Love Lights. No renaming, not even a separate name for the permanent line.

### Tagline
- Keep "Making the Season Bright" on the logo ribbon (it has equity everywhere).
- Lead line recommendation: **"The Season, Handled."**
- Proof line under it: **"Grinch-Proof Guarantee: a dark section fixed in 48 hours, or that month is free. In writing."**
- Retire the competing slogans on gear ("Holiday Lighting Professionals," "We Hang Christmas Lights," "Lighting For Any Occasion").
- Test the lead line on a yard sign and the next 50 estimate emails before committing it to a vehicle wrap.

### The guarantee name (needs your fact-check before anything ships)
The site already calls it the **"Grinch-Proof Guarantee"** (24 to 48 hours) plus a separate "Stay-Lit Guarantee." Recommendation: make "Grinch-Proof Guarantee" the one official name everywhere, retire "Stay-Lit."
- Open question to confirm: is the "or that month is free" penalty actually written into your contract, and is the exact fix window 24 or 48 hours? Confirm before this goes on a truck. It is a money-and-promise item, worth getting exactly right.
- Minor note: "Grinch-Proof" is fun and mass-market, a slight tonal pull against the premium crimson-and-gold world. Memorable enough to keep, but that is the reason it might ever feel too cute for the status customer.

### Vertical naming
Pick one word for the year-round product and use it everywhere. Recommend "Permanent Lighting." Drop "Architectural" (business card) and "Permanent LED" (site) as alternates.

### Palette (reconciles all three current looks)
- Deep crimson #8E1F14, primary brand color
- Warm cream #F6EFE2, type on crimson, signage, the uniform shirt
- Foil gold #C9A24B, thin accents and borders only (the thread already in both good looks)
- Evergreen #1E4634, support and uniforms
- Night navy/black #101014, background behind photos only
- Retire: bright fair red/green, snow-cap effects, clip-art bulbs and houses.

### Type
- Headlines: editorial serif, the family already in the 2025 lookbook (Playfair Display or similar).
- Wordmark and embroidered names: one retro sign-painter script. Free stand-ins that work: Yellowtail or Pacifico. Upgrade later to a licensed or custom one-off so no other Long Island company shares your exact letters. (Lobster is off the table, too common.)
- Body: clean humanist sans.

### Logo system
- Keep the wreath badge for signage, certifications, invoices, and a small door crest on vehicles.
- Add a retro script wordmark for vehicles, apparel, yard signs, social headers.
- The name does not change. This is an addition, not a replacement.

### Photography (your strongest existing asset, protect it)
- Real YLL installs only. Night or dusk. Warm-white-led, color as the accent.
- One house per frame in ads. Galleries can grid.
- People: crew in uniform, homeowners at reveal moments, hands plugging in. No stock.

### The two ownable visual signatures
1. The reveal photo (the customer's own lit house on install night): both the marketing hero image and the gift you send. Own it and you own the emotional peak of the category.
2. The home ornament (custom ornament of the customer's actual house): a recurring visual motif, not just a December gift. Anchors the holiday card, a site section, maybe a seasonal sub-mark. Shows up in their home every Christmas for years.

## The uniform system (biggest new work from this session)

Reference brand Naldo admired: Pink's Window Services (retro 1950s service-crew Americana, script wordmark, crew photos). The YLL version is built distinct, not a copy.

### Three tiers for three moments
1. **Crew** (does the work): 1950s service-crew look.
   - Cream heavyweight work shirt, crimson script first name over the left pocket, small wreath patch on the sleeve.
   - Crimson quilted chore jacket, cream script "Yule Love Lights" across the back with "Long Island" under it. This is the hero piece and the one people photograph, because November and December are jacket weather.
   - Evergreen work pants.
   - Crimson beanie with a cream pom (winter), crimson cap (fall installs).
   - Summer short-sleeve cream work shirt, and a restrained evergreen sublimated performance tee for hot days.
   - Clean boots or clean sneakers, crew's choice. Do not copy Pink's matching-Converse move.
2. **Estimator** (wins trust): dressier than the crew.
   - Crimson sweater vest over a cream collared shirt, dark chinos, a small gold wreath lapel pin. Reads "the manager who cares," not a laborer.
3. **Owner statement** (makes the impact, for events and big pitches):
   - A tailored crimson blazer with gold buttons, or a deep-green velvet dinner jacket, with an evergreen tartan bow tie or pocket square.
   - This is the answer to "I want a Christmas suit that screams Christmas." The tailored version screams Christmas AND stays premium. The novelty version (sequins, light-up tie, ugly-sweater print) reads costume and breaks the whole brand, so skip it. Avoid a Santa hat for the same reason.

### The uniform ritual
On install night, when the display first turns on, the crew lines up at the curb for one photo with the family in front of the lit house. That photo is the reveal photo. The uniform is designed to look right in it.

### Business-card titles
Keep the "cheer titles" on business cards only (Estimator of Holiday Cheer, etc.). Shirts stay first-name only; titles on fabric tip into costume.

### Hats considered
Rope trucker cap (crew, summer casual), structured ball cap (have it), cuffed pom beanie (have it), evergreen watch cap (no pom), wool tartan flat cap (estimator, vintage host, the dressy home run), felt hat with tartan band and holly sprig (owner statement). A crimson wool trapper is the cold-weather statement, not a Santa hat.

### Pants shopping (real products, one green family)
Anchor everyone on Dickies 874 in Hunter Green. Add the Dickies flannel-lined version (or Carhartt B111 washed duck in Moss) for December. Add a lightweight ripstop pant only once permanent installs pick up. Buy one of each first and check the greens against the crimson jacket in daylight before a bulk order.

## Era exploration (the deeper "what could make an impact" question)

Four eras were weighed. The recommendation is a three-layer split, not one era:
- **1950s service crew** for the working uniform (the only era that functions on a ladder in December).
- **1930s grand hotel** for the dressy tier (crimson and gold, doorman-adjacent, matches the lookbook).
- **Victorian lamplighter** as the story, not the clothes (the brand story, the December card, the ornament, one hero image, maybe one full-costume event appearance a year). It is the most ownable idea you have and it earns the "Yule" in the name. Nobody in the industry owns it.

If you ever want to commit everything to a single era instead, that is a clean alternative path to revisit.
